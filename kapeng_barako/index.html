Kapeng Barako

The bold coffee of the Philippines

What it is

Kapeng Barako is a coffee from the Philippines, best known for its strong, robust flavor and pungent aroma. It is a local favorite, especially in its home province of Batangas, and is prized by people who want a coffee with a deep, intense character.

A brief history

Spanish friars introduced coffee to the Philippines in the 1740s, planting it in the lowlands of Lipa, Batangas. Barako is a Liberica variety, a coffee type distinct from the more common Arabica and Robusta. From Lipa it spread across Batangas, which became known for its coffee.

In the 1800s the Philippines became one of the world's major coffee producers, and Barako was exported as far as San Francisco and parts of Europe.

How to brew it

Barako is strong, so a little goes a long way. A common starting point is about 1 part ground coffee to 15 parts water by weight, adjusted to taste.

Method: drip or French press
  1. Use freshly roasted beans and grind them coarse for a French press, or medium for a drip machine or pour-over.
  2. Heat fresh water to just off the boil, around 90 to 95 °C (195 to 200 °F).
  3. Drip or pour: add the coffee to the filter, pour a small amount of water to wet the grounds, wait about 30 seconds, then pour the rest slowly. French press: add the coffee and water together, stir gently, steep about 4 minutes, then press the plunger down slowly.
  4. Pour into a warm cup right away. Serve it black or with a little sugar or milk, since barako is bold enough to stand up to them.

Tip: for a milder cup, use less coffee or shorten the brew time. For a stronger one, add a little more coffee rather than brewing longer, which can make it bitter.
